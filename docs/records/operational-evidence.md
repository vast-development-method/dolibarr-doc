# Which stored records drive an operation?

The [installation boundary register](installation-boundaries.md) identifies 23 tables whose names can be mistaken for the records used by a business process. One table records optional diagnostic activity. One is a retired authorization-state store. **The other 21 are schema-only for the standard business operations described below.** Their fields, defaults and constraints are part of the [physical model](../relational-model/README.md). A rebuild can reproduce each operation using the active records identified here.

The classification covers the built-in application, operator actions, services, background jobs and command-line workers. It accounts for object-to-table declarations, type-specific association maps, module import/export and dictionary declarations, and table names composed when an operation runs. Across the 21 tables, 18 names occur only in database setup material. Outside setup, the other three occur as a screen title for online signing, a comment on a party merge, or comments on employee fields. None selects a reserved table for a business read or write. Foreign keys, schema upgrades and database-maintained modification times still apply as declared. Generic backup and restore, database administration and separately installed modules have their own access to physical data.

## Finance and accounting

| Table | What its schema represents | What the operator's action actually reads or writes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `accounting_analytic_axis` | An entity-scoped analytic dimension with code, label, active flag and audit fields. Code and entity form a unique key. | Ordinary account maintenance selects a chart and edits `accounting_account`. Reporting classifications use account/category relationships. Journal posting does not require an axis, create one or select a category through one. |
| `accounting_analytic_account` | An analytic account with required `fk_axis`, an active flag and optional effective dates. Code is unique within the entity across axes. An upgraded database can enforce an axis foreign key that the fresh accounting setup omits. | The account assigned to a bill or expense is a chart row in `accounting_account`. The assignment and transfer operations do not look up an analytic account or use `date_start` and `date_end` from this table to admit a journal leg. |
| `accounting_analytic_distribution` | The pair `sourcetype` and `fk_source_line` identifies a source. `fk_analytic_account` references an analytic account. Percentage defaults to 100 and amount is optional. | The source journal groups eligible document amounts by accounting treatment and writes bookkeeping legs. It does not convert the percentage into money or distribute a rounding remainder through this table. Two stored 60% and 40% allocations therefore have no effect on ordinary posting or reversal. [Source journal rules](../specification/accounting/source-journal-postings.md) |
| `accounting_balance_snapshot` | Dated debit and credit totals with fiscal year, entity, account and optional subledger. | Balance reporting groups `accounting_bookkeeping` by account in the current entity. In subledger mode it also groups by nonempty subledger account and label. Opening figures come from postings selected before the opening boundary. A snapshot row is neither an opening voucher nor an authoritative report total. |
| `accounting_bookkeeping_piece` | A separate heading with entity/reference identity, posting date, status and validation/closing users. | Posted legs carry `accounting_bookkeeping.piece_num`. The writer reuses the piece of a matching document when applicable or allocates a number, then stores that number on the leg. To reconstruct a piece, follow the legs in the entity. Posting, matching and reporting do not create or close a heading in this table. |
| `accounting_groups_account` | A generated row ID joins an accounting account and an accounting category. | Ordinary account maintenance stores `fk_accounting_category` on the account. Multi-report classification uses `accounting_category_account`, joining `fk_accounting_account` to `fk_accounting_category`. Assigning the same account to that category a second time returns “already assigned”. Removal deletes the active bridge row. |

## Banking, procurement and claims

| Table | What its schema represents | What the operator's action actually reads or writes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `bank_import` | A candidate source transaction with institution ID, original and normalized data, debit/credit, bank and value dates, and a possible duplicate reference. | The cash ledger and statement use posted `bank` movements for a bank account. No built-in import settlement worker promotes or rejects rows from this table, and the stored `fitid` does not implement duplicate suppression in the cash ledger. Importing through a separately configured feed requires that feed's own reconciliation rules. |
| `bank_record` | A statement header carrying a reference, bank-account link, period and creation/validation times. | Ordinary reconciliation sets `bank.rappro`, `num_releve` and, when marking a movement reconciled, `fk_user_rappro`. A statement groups movements for the account and statement reference. Reconciling a movement does not create a statement header. |
| `bank_record_link` | A bridge from `fk_bank_record` to `fk_bank_import`, both mandatory foreign keys. | The bridge joins two other reserved structures. Neither endpoint identifies a posted bank movement. Three movements reconciled to one statement are three `bank` rows carrying the appropriate account and reference. Their selection requires zero rows in this bridge. |
| `commande_fournisseur_log` | A dated supplier-order history row with order, status, user and comment. One supported database also updates its modification time when such a row is edited. | Validation updates `commande_fournisseur.ref`, `fk_statut`, `date_valid` and `fk_user_valid` under the draft-state condition and raises `ORDER_SUPPLIER_VALIDATE` when events are enabled. Order submission updates the header and emits its own event. Subscribed actions can maintain their own history. Neither the order action nor the modification-time trigger inserts a row in this log. |
| `paymentexpensereport_expensereport` | A payment-to-claim allocation with amount, currency, exchange rate and currency amount. | A reimbursement owns its direct `payment_expensereport.fk_expensereport` link. Fetch returns that claim and the payment amount. The payment row's `fk_bank` points to its bank movement. Two instalments on one claim produce two payment rows with the same claim key. The standard payment operation does not fan one row out across claims through the declared bridge. [Expense and salary disbursements](../expense-accounting/employee-payment-registers.md) |

## Catalogue, dispatch and people

| Table | What its schema represents | What the operator's action actually reads or writes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `c_product_thirdparty_relation_type` | An active code and label for classifying a generic product-to-party relationship. | The supplier price chooser resolves eligible supplier-price records. No type from this dictionary is required to offer a product for a supplier, choose a quantity tier or merge a party. |
| `product_thirdparty` | A product, party and type with effective dates, status, optional project and notes. | A supplier item or price uses `product_fournisseur_price.fk_product`, `fk_soc`, `ref_fourn`, `quantity` and pricing fields. These are the records a price enquiry reads. The standard party-merge map does not register the generic relationship table, so a separate writer cannot rely on that merge to revise its links. [Supplier price selection](../registers-operations/supplier-prices.md) |
| `element_categorie` | A category and element ID with no element-type discriminator. | The category object resolves the business type, selects its typed association table and inserts, reads or deletes the pair there. A product assignment uses `categorie_product`. A label on a supplier/customer uses the corresponding party association. Equal numeric IDs in two business families are insufficient to establish a common membership. |
| `expedition_package` | A ranked parcel under a shipment, with package type, declared value, dimensions, weight, goods designation and lift requirement. | Shipping method, tracking number, dimensions and weight belong to `expedition`. Shipped quantities are allocated via `expeditiondet` and lot movements through `expeditiondet_batch`. These relationships identify what shipped. They do not record a parcel-to-line or parcel-to-serial allocation. The standard dispatch does not aggregate ranked parcel rows into its weight. |
| `c_recruitment_origin` | A source-of-application dictionary with a code, label and active flag. The candidature has a nullable `fk_recruitment_origin`. | The ordinary job-application editor uses its active field definitions, which omit the origin picker. It can create and process the candidature without opening this dictionary, and it does not use the origin row to decide its status. A value supplied separately is a stored reference, not a recruiter action in this workflow. |
| `user_employment` | A possible dated series of job, salary, supplementary salary and weekly-hour terms for a user. | Current account terms are read from and edited on `user.job`, `salary`, `salaryextra` and `weeklyhours`. A salary obligation and its disbursements have their own `salary` and `payment_salary` rows. A change of weekly hours neither closes an employment-period row here nor revises an existing salary payment. |

## Notifications, signatures and surveys

| Table | What its schema represents | What the operator's action actually reads or writes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `notify_def_object` | A scheduled object-specific notification identifying its target object, user, channel and time. | Event subscriptions use `notify_def` to find eligible contacts or users. Calendar reminders use `actioncomm_reminder` for the event and reminder details. Changing a reservation or recording a reminder does not queue an object notification in this table. |
| `onlinesignature` | A potential signature record with object identity, signer details, IP address and file path. | The signing handler selects the owning document type. For a proposal it can update signed status, signature date, remote address and the name if supplied. A shipment uses its own signed-status operation and shipping modification event. Signed files can be indexed through the owning document. An on-screen signature label does not identify a row in this table. |
| `opensurvey_formquestions` | A linked question with answer choices under a survey. | The text and date survey creation flows put choices in `opensurvey_sondage.sujet`. The stored order determines which position a vote addresses. The form-question table supplies no questions to these workflows. |
| `opensurvey_user_formanswers` | Per-question answer data associated with a respondent and question. | The normal voting flow writes the respondent's encoded selections to `opensurvey_user_studs.reponses`. Editing a vote changes that row. Reading results filters the respondent rows by `id_sondage`. A respondent choosing three options has one ballot row, with no per-question answer rows required. [Survey selection](../registers-operations/surveys.md) |

## Retired state and optional logging

**`oauth_state`.** The standard database-backed authorization flow looks up `oauth_token` by service and permitted entity. It updates the token row's state, or creates a token row with state when absent. The callback reads that state from the same storage adapter. The standalone state table is deprecated. Session-based storage provided by another adapter has its own persistence semantics.

**`ai_request_log`.** The assistant and tool-service entry points each check their enablement settings. Writing the diagnostic row also requires request logging. Each call attempts one insert for the current entity and user, recording query, tool, provider, elapsed time, confidence, outcome, error and raw request/response. Raw input and output over 60,000 characters are shortened and marked. The optional agenda and hash columns are unused by this writer. The administrative viewer filters by permitted entity. Deletion and a separately configured positive retention interval can remove rows. This table therefore has a concrete operating role under its feature settings.

## Worked checks on the active records

The following results were reproduced by calling the business operations against an isolated relational store containing only the active tables needed for each example. All 21 reserved tables were absent. These checks exercise the named read or write at the business-method boundary. They do not depend on a browser, mail delivery or third-party integration.

| Operation and starting data | Observed result |
| --- | --- |
| Current entity has postings to account 411 with debits 100 and 40, and credit 30. A different entity has debit 999. | The general balance shows one account with debit 140 and credit 30. Subledger mode returns two debtors. Reporting reads the posting table directly. |
| Assign account 7 to reporting category 3, repeat the assignment, then remove it. | The initial assignment creates one active category link. The repeat reports that it already exists. Removing it leaves no membership. |
| Mark bank movement 12 as reconciled to statement S-4 as user 8. | The same movement now contains reconciliation flag 1, statement S-4 and reconciling user 8. |
| Read expense payment 17 for 60, linked directly to claim 53 and bank movement 12. | The payment reader returns claim 53, amount 60 and bank movement 12. No allocation bridge participates. |
| Read survey P1 with two encoded ballots, `101` and `010`. Another survey has a third ballot. | The result contains only the two P1 ballots with their original positional responses. |
