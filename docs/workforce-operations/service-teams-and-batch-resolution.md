# Service teams, private conversations and batch resolution

A support case, a technician's intervention and a service line on a maintenance contract have separate states and authors. A dispatch supervisor can resolve a case, a contract manager can end coverage, and a billing clerk can invoice completed work. The screens offer different choices depending on which of these records the operator has selected.

## Who sees the work and its conversation?

An internal user needs ticket read access to open the case list. The ordinary entity filter applies. The employee's *mine* queue includes tickets they are assigned and, when assigned-only visibility is off, tickets they created. With assigned-only on, the restricted list includes the assigned employee's rows. A nonadministrator can fall under that filter even without selecting *mine*. An external account's party supplies its party filter. The ticket, messaging and document views also have their own record-level gates. In particular, the staff conversation and documents distinguish the assigned internal employee from an operator with management authority.

A customer's public conversation displays the ticket's eligible messages after the public record-access decision. Each stored activity has a code, date, owner or author and message body. The message loader selects ticket activities by the matching type and record ID and sorts them by event time descending. A code beginning with the private-ticket-message prefix marks an activity private, including a private message sent by email. The public view explicitly calls the conversation renderer with private messages disabled. The same internal body column can hold a customer-visible message or private staff correspondence. Projecting the column without its activity classification loses the visibility decision. A delivery to a named mailbox and permission to read a message from the customer-facing thread are separate outcomes.

For example, a technician writes “Replacement installed” for the customer, then an internal note about the replacement cost. Both can enter the activity history of the same ticket. The public conversation shows the first. A private message that was emailed to a staff recipient retains its private thread classification. The fact of an email does not make it customer-visible.

## Close and reopen a selection of tickets

| Question | Bulk close | Bulk reopen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Menu authority | Ticket write. The menu offers a confirmation step with an email choice. | Ticket write. |
| Starting population | Selected records that can be fetched. The business close treats an already solved or abandoned case as a successful no-change for purposes of this loop. | Every selected record must be solved or abandoned. A row in another state stops processing. |
| State and fields | New closures use the ordinary solved outcome, set progress to 100 and record a close timestamp. An already final record is left in its existing state. | Uses the generic state operation to set **assigned** and invoke the ticket modification event. It does not select a new technician, reset the completion percentage or clear the previous close timestamp in that update. |
| Other records | Optional intervention workflow acts on linked service visits. Closure also publishes the ticket-close event for each newly closed case. | The reopen branch contains no reversal of linked interventions and no second close notification. |
| Batch result | Counts each nonnegative close result, including a previously final case. A missing selected row or a negative close result rolls back the outer transaction. | Counts successful transitions. A missing, invalid-state or failed transition rolls back the outer transaction. |

When a new closure emits the close event, its standard email subscriber can send an administrative notification to the configured mailbox. On the customer side, it collects the ticket's party and internal contacts. With no contacts, the configured close-notification option and associated party can supply a fallback address. A selected, linked and active contact with an email takes priority; bulk close with affirmative mail confirmation can otherwise use all linked addresses. Disabling ticket email in the object context inhibits the subscriber's direct notices. The affirmative mail choice and the administrative mailbox are independent inputs. A notification already delivered during one case's closure survives a later rollback of the database batch.

Consider three selected cases. One is already abandoned, one is assigned and has an open field intervention, and one is already solved. Successful bulk close can report three modified records while only the assigned case is newly solved. It generates the close event for that case. Bulk reopen of these three transitions each to assigned. The intervention that was completed by the close remains completed. If the same reopen selection includes a fourth ticket that is still waiting, the invalid starting state causes rollback of the whole reopen batch rather than committing only the first three.

## Contract, service-line and intervention lists

| Selected objects | Built-in list actions | Where to make a service decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contract headers | Regenerate documents, merge PDFs, send by mail and, with contract deletion rights, delete. | The card exposes opening or closing a line or all lines of that single contract under the separate activation and deactivation rights. |
| Contract service lines across agreements | The built-in selection menu registers no bulk activation or closure. | Open the owning agreement or use its service with the contract and line identifiers. |
| Interventions across customers | Regenerate documents, merge PDFs, delete with deletion rights, and apply a tag when classification is enabled and the actor can create categories. | A single card handles validation, signature, completion, billing classification and reopening. |
| Linked intervention during case resolution | No separate intervention batch needs to be chosen. | With the intervention module and the ticket-close workflow enabled, ticket closure loads the linked visit IDs and performs the configured transitions. |

The per-contract collective operation has a different grain from a selection of contracts. Activating a contract's services skips already open lines, can open initial or closed ones, and validates a draft header after the line operations. Closing them skips already closed lines and uses a common current time on the others. The group of line updates belongs to one agreement transaction. A contract with twelve lines can be activated in one operation. Twelve separate agreements on the contract list cannot be activated by applying the document-regeneration action. The [contract service guide](../specification/people-and-services/contract-services-and-work-templates.md) gives the service periods, dates and expiry calculations.

The contract-card move-line action requires contract editing and access to the target contract. The selected line must belong to the current contract. The target must be a different agreement for the same party. It changes the existing line's parent and calls its update with event suppression. In a service renewal this can preserve line identity while changing which agreement owns it. A move to a contract for a different customer fails the relationship decision.

Completion of a linked intervention also has a distinct event contract. Direct completion on the intervention card uses the close operation, which writes closure state, intervention date and modifying actor and publishes the intervention-close event. The ticket workflow validates a draft visit and sets state 3 through the generic classifier. For an intervention this generic transition emits the *classify done* event. It changes the state field and has no corresponding write of the date and modifying actor from the dedicated close operation. Both paths can produce a closed visit while yielding different evidence for downstream reporting or notifications. Reopening the visit from its own card sets it back to validated. Classification as billed changes the intervention's state. It creates no customer payment or invoice by itself.
